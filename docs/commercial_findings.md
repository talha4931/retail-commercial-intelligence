# Commercial findings

This note records the key outputs from the project's completed SQL analysis. These are descriptive findings from the historical dataset, not causal conclusions or forecasts.

## Executive picture

- Sales revenue: £20,476,260.45
- Orders: 40,077
- Average order value: £510.92
- Units sold: 11,205,148
- Identifiable customers: 5,878
- Cancellation value: £1,462,050.61

Sales KPIs include only records classified as SALE. Cancellation value is shown separately.

## Revenue quality and customer behaviour

- Returning customers generated 72.7% of identified and unidentified November 2011 revenue; new customers generated 4.2%, while unidentified-customer transactions represented 23.1%.
- Compared with November 2010, November 2011 new-customer revenue fell 53.3%, while returning-customer revenue rose 6.0%.
- 449 customers were classified as high-value at risk under the project's rule-based segmentation (at least 5 orders, at least £1,000 historical revenue, and no purchase for at least 91 days).
- Those high-value inactive customers account for approximately £1.76m of historical revenue. This is not a forecast of recoverable revenue or revenue that will necessarily be lost.
- The top 100 identifiable customers account for 37.55% of identifiable-customer revenue.

## Product and order concentration

- Excluding operational stock codes M, DOT and POST, the top 100 merchandise products account for 29.25% of merchandise revenue.
- Orders above £1,000 represent 8.98% of orders and 45.91% of sales revenue.
- The top 100 orders account for 9.20% of sales revenue. High-value orders matter, but revenue is not solely dependent on a handful of invoices.

## Geographic performance

- The UK accounts for 85.51% of merchandise revenue.
- UK and EIRE together account for 88.73%.
- France and Germany showed year-on-year growth in the like-for-like January-November comparison; EIRE declined.
- Netherlands and Australia revenue was highly concentrated among a small number of identified customers. These small customer bases make percentage comparisons volatile.

## Cancellations and data caveats

- Cancellation value totals £1.46m, but this includes large non-merchandise and unusual bulk adjustments. It should not be interpreted as a confirmed lost-sales rate.
- Some country cancellation-to-sales ratios are based on small revenue bases and can be unstable.
- A single very large Paper Craft transaction materially affects some product and cancellation rankings; sensitivity analysis showed that overall product concentration conclusions changed only modestly.

## Recommended management questions

1. How can the business protect high-value repeat customers?
2. What explains the decline in new-customer revenue?
3. Which high-value orders and customer relationships should be monitored?
4. Which international markets show repeatable growth rather than a small-customer concentration effect?
5. Which cancellation patterns reflect recurring product or process issues?

## Limitations

The dataset does not include product costs, marketing spend, acquisition channels, inventory availability or cancellation reasons. The analysis therefore cannot establish profit, marketing effectiveness, customer lifetime value, causal drivers, inventory optimisation or investment returns. Segmentation thresholds are project-defined rather than industry standards.
